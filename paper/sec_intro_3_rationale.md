## The missing control, the question, and the objectives

### The gap

The operational habit in peptide design is short: fold the designed sequence against its intended
receptor, read the interface confidence, and treat a high value as evidence that the peptide
engages the target. The habit is not absurd — confidence-based filtering raised de novo binder
success rates nearly ten-fold on protein binders [@bennett2023] — but the inference it licenses is
narrower than the use made of it. Interface confidence estimates how well a complex is modelled
given that a complex forms; the model is never offered the option of declining. In a large all-by-
all screen of human genome-maintenance proteins, standard AlphaFold-Multimer confidence does not
reliably separate genuine interactions from an abundance of false positives [@schmid2025]; for
peptides, confidence metrics correlate poorly with experimental binding affinities across 261
curated experimentally resolved complexes [@zhai2025]; and on pMHC-II binding cores, AlphaFold-
based methods that recall true binders well still misclassify shuffled peptides as binders
[@ko2026].

The control that would test this reading is cheap and usually absent: fold the same receptor
against sequences of identical amino-acid composition and different order, drawn from the
candidate's own residues. Negative controls in co-folding screens are typically non-cognate pairs
generated by all-by-all enumeration — the ~40,000 predictions from an all-by-all matrix of nearly
300 human genome-maintenance proteins are one such screen [@schmid2025] — and pairings built this
way leave composition, length and alignment depth free to vary with sequence order. A per-
candidate permutation null removes the composition and length confounds by construction, and
alignment depth was handled separately by running the screen both without an MSA and with a full
one.

### Why composition is the right thing to hold fixed

Composition is the first thing a sequence model can exploit and the last thing a designer intends
it to respond to. In sequence search, a suite of refinements raised retrieval accuracy from
ROC 0.758 ± 0.005 to 0.895 ± 0.003 over 103 expert-curated queries, and the single modification
accounting for the majority of that gain was the use, for each database sequence, of a
position-specific scoring system tuned to that sequence's amino-acid composition [@schaffer2001] —
much of what looked like homology signal was composition signal. Sequence-only
property prediction shows the same ceiling: in blind assessment the best disorder predictor reached
F = 0.483 on the full dataset and F = 0.792 once bona fide structured regions were filtered out,
while the disordered *binding* regions that contact a partner reached only F = 0.231 [@necci2021].
The compositional property is tractable; the specificity built on it is not.

What a permutation preserves determines what the test can detect [@jiang2008]. A random permutation
of the candidate's own residues destroys every motif while holding net charge, hydrophobic fraction,
glycine/serine content and length constant — precisely the bulk properties a co-folding model can
exploit without recognising anything. For chimeric peptides that are manual concatenations of
published motifs joined by GGGGS linkers — built without a generative model, without sequence
optimisation and without structure-based design software — this is the informative null: if a
candidate cannot beat permutations of itself, its motif content is contributing nothing the model
can see. The chemistry literature reached the same conclusion the hard way: property-matched decoy
sets were built to defeat artificial enrichment and analogue bias [@mysinger2012], deep models
trained on them were later shown to profit from the analogue and decoy bias hidden in the dataset
rather than learned protein–ligand recognition [@chen2019], and across seven widely used
ligand-based benchmarks the degree of training–validation redundancy correlated with measured
performance, so reported accuracy there may reflect benchmark overfitting rather than prospective
accuracy [@wallach2018], and when biased targets were removed the count on which Glide succeeded
fell from 30 of 102 to 5 of 47 [@chaput2016]. Tightening the null lowers the score [@imrie2021].

### The question

Does a current co-folding predictor assign systematically higher interface confidence to chimeric
peptides constructed against cognition-related CNS targets than to composition-matched shuffles of
those same peptides' residues, folded against the same receptor domains?

### Objectives and the design decisions they force

Four decisions follow from taking that question seriously rather than rhetorically.

*A gate before the screen was allowed to count.* Throughout, *gate* names a pre-specified pass/fail
precondition on the instrument — a benchmark the pipeline had to clear before the screen would be
read at all — and not gating in any physiological sense. A null result is informative only if the
instrument can resolve the quantity at issue, so interface reconstruction was benchmarked against 16
experimentally determined X-ray peptide–receptor complexes before any candidate was scored: 62%
CAPRI-acceptable, median DockQ 0.36, median fnat 0.518, and Spearman rho between ipTM and DockQ of
0.8. The gate establishes that interfaces are recovered acceptably more often than not — 62%
acceptable leaves 38% not acceptable, by subtraction — which is enough to make a null interpretable
and says nothing about competence at discriminating binders; pose accuracy and the discrimination of
binders from decoys are separable axes [@hoegen2025]. Recovering the interface at all is itself
difficult [@johanssonakhe2022; @bret2024].

*A measured noise floor before any difference was interpreted.* The sampler is stochastic, and a
single run's difference is uninterpretable without its run-to-run spread — the same point that
makes a P value from one experiment a poor guide to what a replicate would return [@halsey2015].
Re-seeding gave an ipTM standard deviation of 0.14943, a complex pLDDT standard deviation of
2.6615, and a minimum-interface-PAE standard deviation of 4.6157. Any candidate–decoy gap smaller
than that floor is not a finding.

*Pre-registration under a SHA-256 hash before data were seen.* Analytic flexibility exercised
after the data are visible inflates false positives far above nominal rates [@simmons2011;
@ioannidis2005], and it constrains only to the extent the registered document is specific enough
to be violated, which most are not [@van2024]; even where it is, adherence is patchy — of 27
studies carrying the Preregistered badge only two deviated from plan in no respect, and nine
disclosed none of their deviations [@claesen2021]. Here, 8 studies covering 25
hypotheses were registered under a SHA-256 hash before their data existed, with 5 hypotheses
decided by a test statistic and 19 by a pre-fixed threshold. Multiplicity correction was applied
only to the former: a step-down adjustment can convert rejections into non-rejections and nothing
else [@aickin1996], and threshold criteria carry no p-value, so there is no family for a
p-value adjustment to act on; where adjustment does apply, what counts as a family is a property of
the report rather than of the data [@perneger1998], and routine adjustment buys reduced type I
error at the price of inflated type II error [@rothman1990].

*A screen-level null distinct from the per-candidate one.* "Better than its own decoys" and "any
candidate better than the null's best" are different questions [@storey2003; @elias2007]. With 10
decoys per candidate the per-candidate null probability of beating all of them is 0.0909, so
across 13 candidates the expected number of winners is 1.182.

### What would have counted as a positive answer

A positive answer required a paired designed-minus-decoy difference exceeding the measured sampler
noise floor, together with a count of candidates beating all of their own decoys exceeding the
binomial screen-level expectation. Neither was obtained. The paired difference was 0.0009 ipTM
units against a seed-to-seed standard deviation of 0.14943 — noise exceeding signal by a factor
above 160, by division — with Cohen's dz of 0.0057; 2 of 13 candidates beat all their decoys
against 1.182 expected, and an outcome at least this extreme has probability 0.3338 under the
null. The answer was negative.

Two caveats fix its scope. It is not evidence that these peptides fail to bind: no binding
measurement was made, and a non-significant difference is not evidence of absence absent a pre-
specified equivalence bound [@altman1995; @lakens2017]. Nor is it a verdict on the predictor in
general. It reports what this predictor's interface confidence did on this construct set under
this null: a within-candidate, composition-matched effect size. Under-controlled evaluation is a
general failure mode of machine-learning-based science, where leakage has been documented in 17
fields across 294 papers [@kapoor2023].
