**Background**

Peptide design routinely folds a candidate against its intended receptor and reads high interface confidence as evidence of engagement. But that confidence estimates how well a complex is modelled given that a complex forms; the predictor is never offered the option of declining. The control that would test it — the same receptor folded against sequences of identical composition and scrambled residue order — is cheap and usually absent.

**Methods**

Thirteen chimeric peptides — manual concatenations of published short linear motifs, motif-like segments built to resemble them without deriving from any specific one, and one de novo helix, joined by GGGGS linkers, with no generative model, no sequence optimisation and no structure-based design software involved — were co-folded with Boltz-2 v2.2.1 against seven receptor domains drawn from a sixteen-target cognition registry. Each was compared with ten decoys built as random permutations of its own residues, which hold composition and length fixed and destroy only residue order. Decoys were run against the same construct with identical settings and full receptor alignment. Candidates, decoy construction, endpoints and decision rules were registered under a SHA-256 hash before any data were seen.

**Results**

Mean designed ipTM was 0.6287 against a mean decoy ipTM of 0.6278: paired difference 0.0009, Cohen's dz 0.0057, paired t-test p = 0.98. Two of thirteen candidates exceeded all ten of their own decoys; against 1.182 expected under a per-candidate null probability of 0.0909, P(X >= 2) = 0.3338 — an exploratory screen-level calculation made after the per-candidate results were seen, not a pre-registered test. Re-seeding the sampler on identical inputs gave an ipTM standard deviation of 0.14943 pooled over six constructs (per-construct values 0.0689 to 0.2344), placing the paired difference more than two orders of magnitude below that floor. The pipeline was characterised before the screen was read: over sixteen X-ray peptide-receptor complexes it returned 62% CAPRI-acceptable models pooled — 0.875 on complexes deposited before the model's training cutoff and 0.375 on those deposited after it — a median DockQ of 0.36, and a Spearman rho between ipTM and DockQ of 0.8.

**Conclusions**

The result licenses one claim, and it is the Conclusions' own: ipTM from this predictor, on this construct class, does not rank manually concatenated motif chimeras above permutations of their own residues, so a screen ordering such constructs by that quantity cannot be shown to be ordering anything above its own seed-to-seed noise. It does not show that these peptides fail to bind: no assay of any kind was performed, and it is not a demonstration of equivalence, no smallest effect of interest having been fixed in advance. Nor is it a verdict on the predictor: pose accuracy and the discrimination of binders from decoys are separable axes, and the calibration available here cannot separate a real null from an instrument that cannot resolve an interface it has not already been shown. One recommendation follows: a per-candidate composition-matched null and the sampler's seed-to-seed spread belong alongside confidence-based rankings of this kind — designed candidates scored by co-folding interface confidence — at eleven predictions per candidate instead of one.
