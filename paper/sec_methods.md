## Methods

### Target selection and construct definition

Sixteen human proteins were entered into a target registry before any candidate was designed. Selection was by mechanistic relevance to cognition rather than by tractability, and each entry carries an explicit annotation of the human genetic support for the target: one strong, two moderate, four weak, two contradictory and seven none. That annotation is part of the registry record, and it shows which targets were carried forward despite weak support.

Domain assignments follow the primary structural literature where it exists: the Trk Ig2/d5 modules from the NGF-TrkA and NT-4/5-TrkB co-crystals [@wiesmann1999; @banfield2001], the acetylcholinesterase gorge from the Torpedo structure and its human counterparts [@sussman1991; @cheung2012], the alpha-7 nicotinic extracellular domain from the cryo-EM series [@noviello2021], the GluN2A and GluN2B amino-terminal domains from the GluN1-GluN2 ATD heterodimers [@karakas2011; @romerohernandez2016; @hansen2018], the TREM2 Ig-V ectodomain from the disease-variant structural work [@kober2016], and the TLR4/MD-2 interface from the LPS co-complex [@park2009].

Constructs — the in silico residue spans supplied to the predictor, not expression constructs — were derived from the canonical UniProt sequence of each accession under three fixed rules. Signal peptides were removed. Where the site of interest is extracellular, transmembrane and cytoplasmic segments were removed, so that the modelled chain is the ectodomain or the isolated domain rather than the full-length protein; the two seven-transmembrane targets (P25105, P11229) are the exception, their sites lying inside the helical bundle. Residue numbering followed the canonical UniProt sequence throughout, so that every boundary in the registry and in the run records indexes the canonical entry rather than a construct-local offset.

Construct definitions were reviewed against those rules and corrected where they deviated; the corrected definitions are the ones used for every result reported here. Corrections were made to the registry entry rather than to a downstream copy, so that no analysis could run against a construct the registry did not describe.

### Candidate design

No generative model designed any candidate, and no sequence optimisation or structure-based design software was used at any stage. The candidates are chimeric peptides, manually concatenated from published short linear motifs, motif-like segments built to resemble such motifs without deriving from a specific one, and a single de novo helix, joined by GGGGS linkers of the standard flexible type [@chen2013]. The design logic is that of short linear motifs — much protein regulation runs through short, low-affinity, modular elements that can in principle be excised and recombined [@van2014] — combined with the hotspot argument that a few side chains can carry most of an interface's binding energy [@clackson1995]. Deep-learning design methods such as RFdiffusion or ProteinMPNN [@watson2023; @dauparas2022] were deliberately not used: a sequence optimised against the same network that then scores it cannot be used to ask whether that network reads design content — by which we mean throughout the order-dependent content a design places in a sequence, its motif identities, linker boundaries and the helical-face arrangement of the single helical segment, as distinct from the amino acid composition that a shuffle holds fixed.

Because provenance is the weak point of manual concatenation, the candidate sequences and their source motifs were audited for attribution before the screen was analysed. The motif table holds sixteen entries, of which seven carry a UniProt accession identifying the motif's parent protein; nine do not. Twenty-two distinct fragments are labelled by the design record itself as unattributed, and thirty-one of the thirty-five candidates carry at least one such fragment — by arithmetic, four are free of them. Thirteen candidates entered the full-MSA screen reported here. The audit belongs in the Methods rather than in a limitations paragraph because it constrains any positive result: a candidate whose fragments cannot be traced to a source cannot be said to have inherited affinity from one.

### Composition-matched decoys

The null for each candidate is a set of shuffles of that candidate's own sequence, uniform random permutations of its residues; this per-candidate distribution is what is meant throughout by the composition-matched null, and its members by composition-matched decoys or shuffles. Shuffling holds the multiset of residues constant, fixing length, composition, net charge, hydrophobic fraction and glycine/serine content, and destroys residue order and with it every motif, linker boundary and the helical-face arrangement of the single helical segment. The composition here is identical rather than merely matched, which distinguishes this null from the property-matched decoy sets of ligand virtual screening, whose members are different molecules chosen to resemble the ligand in bulk properties while differing in topology. Preserving composition is not cosmetic: composition alone carries enough signal that composition-based statistics were adopted as a correction in sequence search, one of several refinements that together raised retrieval accuracy [@schaffer2001], and disordered segments are distinguished from ordered ones largely by their amino acid composition [@romero2001], so a null that did not hold it constant would confound design content with amino acid composition. Higher-order shuffles preserving k-let counts are available [@jiang2008] and were not used: for a construct whose designed content is motif-level, the shuffle used here isolates exactly that content.

The virtual-screening literature supplies the standard warning about the opposite failure: decoys separable on bulk properties produce artificial enrichment, and measured discrimination becomes a property of the decoy set rather than of the method [@mysinger2012; @chaput2016; @chen2019; @wallach2018; @imrie2021]. Shuffles of the candidate itself cannot exhibit that particular artefact, since design and decoy share every bulk property by construction, at the cost of being a within-item rather than a between-item null.

Three decoys per candidate were generated and predicted in the single-sequence study, ten per candidate in the full-MSA study. Decoys were predicted against the same target construct, with the same predictor version and settings as the corresponding design, so that the only difference between a designed prediction and its decoys is the order of the peptide's residues.

### Structure prediction

All complexes were predicted with Boltz-2 v2.2.1, run locally on Apple silicon using the MPS backend, with MIT-licensed code and weights. Boltz-1 and Boltz-2 are open reimplementations of the AlphaFold3-generation co-folding architecture; Boltz-1 introduced inference-time steering to suppress non-physical outputs, and Boltz-2 adds an affinity module [@wohlwend2025; @passaro2025]. Two conditions were run: single sequence, with no multiple sequence alignment, and full MSA for the receptor chain. The peptide has no evolutionary record as a construct in either condition.

AlphaFold 3 was not used [@abramson2024]: its code was available by request only and under a non-commercial licence, and its released implementation targets Linux with CUDA, which the local hardware does not provide. AlphaFold Server was not used either — its terms of service prohibit automated protein-ligand and protein-peptide binding prediction, precisely the use this study would have made of it. The AlphaFold Protein Structure Database was used under its CC BY 4.0 licence as an independent comparison for the receptor constructs [@varadi2022]; agreement between AlphaFold DB and Boltz-2 per-residue confidence over matched constructs had median Pearson r 0.7154 in the single-sequence condition and 0.8637 in the full-MSA condition.

The Boltz-2 affinity head was recorded but is never rendered as a thermodynamic quantity. It is a regressor fitted to pooled Ki, Kd, IC50 and EC50 labels; public IC50 values are assay-specific and comparable only under certain conditions, and Ki and IC50 sit on different scales, requiring an offset of roughly a factor of two before they can be pooled at all [@kalliokoski2013]; independent probing of the same head found its binary active/inactive calls insensitive to key binding-site mutations and, in some cases, unchanged under target exchange [@bret2026].

### Confidence and interface measures

The primary endpoint of the screen is ipTM, the interface-restricted predicted TM-score introduced with AlphaFold-Multimer [@evans2021]. Complex pLDDT and PAE were recorded alongside it; both are regressions onto geometric agreement with an experimental structure of the same chain, not probabilities that the modelled state exists in solution [@jumper2021; @varadi2022]. Interface PAE, the predicted aligned error matrix restricted to inter-chain residue pairs, is reported descriptively only: it is a quality estimate whose interpretation is acknowledged to be difficult and which comes with no standard cutoff [@elfmann2023], and no decision in this study rests on an interface PAE cutoff.

Where an experimental reference exists, pose quality was scored with DockQ v2 against the X-ray structure [@mirabello2024]. DockQ combines fnat, ligand RMSD and interface RMSD into a continuous score reproducing the CAPRI incorrect/acceptable/medium/high classification, with DockQ >= 0.23 the operational definition of acceptable [@basu2016b]; v2 adds automatic chain mapping and handling of multiple interfaces [@mirabello2024], and scores are read here against the same CAPRI bins as the earlier literature. The CAPRI bins were built for rigid-body docking of two globular partners [@mendez2003; @lensink2007] and the group that analysed the first CAPRI peptide-protein rounds concluded the criteria needed revision for short ligands, proposing a loosened backbone-RMSD threshold combined with a side-chain-RMSD restriction [@marcu2017], so peptide DockQ values are read here as ordinal.

### Benchmark and control studies

The screen is bracketed by control studies, each pre-registered independently of it.

The interface gate is a benchmark of sixteen X-ray peptide-receptor complexes re-predicted under the candidate pipeline. It returned 62% CAPRI-acceptable models, median DockQ 0.36, median fnat 0.518, and Spearman rho between ipTM and DockQ of 0.8. The gate was pre-specified as the precondition for interpreting the screen at all: a pipeline that could not place known peptides could not be asked about unknown ones. Those sixteen references are deposited structures that may lie within the predictor's training corpus, so the gate establishes that the pipeline can reproduce known placements, not that it generalises to unseen ones [@zhai2025; @li2024]. For scale rather than equivalence: the 62% acceptable rate is close to the 59% (66/112) reported for AlphaFold-Multimer on peptide-protein complexes, while the median DockQ of 0.36 falls below that work's median first-ranked DockQ of 0.47 [@johanssonakhe2022]; PepPCBench reports that such values track training-set similarity, so the two sets are not necessarily comparable [@zhai2025].

The seed-variance study re-ran identical inputs across seeds to measure the sampler's own noise floor: ipTM standard deviation 0.14943, complex pLDDT standard deviation 2.6615, and minimum interface PAE standard deviation 4.6157. Reporting this floor is not optional: a single-seed confidence value is one draw from a distribution, and in massive-sampling experiments on antibody-antigen targets the AlphaFold2 confidence score did not identify the best models within a sampled pool [@raouraoua2025], although pool-median ipTM does track interface difficulty [@raouraoua2026].

The pose-accuracy study asked separately, on the same reference complexes, whether fold and placement succeed or fail together. The AChE affinity benchmark tested whether the affinity head ranks known acetylcholinesterase ligands: Spearman 0.3036, mean absolute error 1.3593 log10 units, fraction within one log unit 0.5333. Because reference-value errors would inflate the error term, it was rerun against corrected references, and the rerun is reported as its own study rather than folded silently into the first. PRODIGY, a contact-based estimator of binding free energy, was run as an orthogonal non-neural interface score and enters the analysis only through a ratio: discrimination ratio 1.4 — the registered name for the between-candidate SD of predicted dG divided by the within-candidate across-seed SD, a signal-to-noise quantity and not the novel-object-recognition index of the same name — bootstrap confidence interval [0.9629, 3.844], an interval that includes 1 and therefore does not establish discrimination; the predicted energies occupy 18% of PRODIGY's calibration range, the span of experimental binding free energies its linear model was fitted on.

### Statistics

Every study was pre-registered internally rather than deposited with an external registry: its protocol was frozen and identified by a SHA-256 hash before its data were seen, fixing candidates, decoy construction, endpoints, decision rules and thresholds, and every downstream artefact records that hash. The hash is taken over a canonical serialisation of the registered plan, so any change to the registered content yields a different digest. The hash makes the plan tamper-evident — it cannot be edited to match a result without the hash changing and the change surfacing in every artefact that cites it — but it is not a third-party timestamp, and priority over the data rests on the archive rather than on an independent registrar. The rationale is the standard one: analytic flexibility exercised after the data are visible raises the real false-positive rate far above nominal [@simmons2011; @ioannidis2005; @tierney2021; @el2022]. Pre-registration constrains only to the extent that the registered document is specific enough to be violated, which audits find is often not the case [@van2024], and where deviations from the plan do occur they usually go undisclosed [@claesen2021]; hashing the protocol is a design claim the reader can check, not a virtue asserted.

Across the slate, eight pre-registered studies posed twenty-five hypotheses: thirteen confirmed, eleven falsified, one not tested. Five were decided by a test statistic and nineteen by a fixed threshold, which by arithmetic accounts for the twenty-four that were tested. That split drives the multiplicity policy. Holm's step-down procedure [@aickin1996] was applied only to the family of genuine test statistics: a threshold comparison carries no p-value and cannot belong to a correction family. Applying a family-wise adjustment to a hypothesis whose claim is failure to reject is backwards in a checkable sense — every step-down adjustment maps a p-value to a value greater than or equal to itself, so correction can only convert rejections into non-rejections, and offers no protection against the error a negative result actually risks [@rothman1990; @perneger1998].

The screen's primary test is a paired comparison of designed against decoy-mean ipTM across the thirteen candidates: mean designed ipTM 0.6287 versus mean decoy 0.6278, paired difference 0.0009, Cohen's dz 0.0057, paired t-test p = 0.98. Effect size, raw difference and the reseeding standard deviation are reported together because a p-value alone cannot show whether a difference is small relative to the instrument's noise [@halsey2015; @greenland2016; @button2013]. The paired difference is far below the sampler's own ipTM standard deviation of 0.14943, and no smallest effect size of interest was pre-specified, so the result is reported as no difference detected at the achieved precision and explicitly not as demonstrated equivalence [@altman1995; @lakens2017].

The screen-level analysis is a separate object from the per-candidate one. Under exchangeability a candidate's probability of beating all ten of its own decoys is 1/11 = 0.0909, so the number of winners across thirteen candidates is binomial with expectation 1.182, which is thirteen times that probability. Two candidates beat all ten of their decoys; P(X >= 2) = 0.3338. The comparison of observed to expected winners, not the winners themselves, is the evidence [@storey2003; @elias2007]. Confidence intervals for ratio statistics, including the PRODIGY discrimination ratio, are non-parametric bootstrap intervals.

Hypothesis H1, the hypothesis that the predictor separates designed candidates from composition-matched shuffles, was falsified in all eleven retained versions of the analysis.

### Reproducibility

Every prediction is stored under a content-addressed run identifier derived from its inputs, so a run cannot be silently replaced by a re-run with different settings, and every derived artefact carries a provenance stamp naming the runs it consumed. A manifest ties registry, design record, pre-registrations and run archive together. This follows the standard prescription for computational reproducibility — portable software packages, containerised execution environments and workflow systems [@gruning2018] — to which content addressing of every run is added here, against a measured baseline in which only a small fraction of published computational analyses re-execute at all, dependency decay and undeclared environment state being the dominant failure modes rather than scientific error [@samuel2024]. Because leakage rather than arithmetic is the usual reason such a result fails to replicate [@kapoor2023; @graber2025; @li2024], the archive retains superseded as well as current records.

### What was not done

No docking, no MM-GBSA, no free-energy perturbation and no molecular dynamics were performed, and no conformational sampling beyond the predictor's own. No wet-lab validation of any kind was done — no binding assay, no cell assay, no animal work — so no candidate here has been shown to bind anything. No blood-brain-barrier permeability was measured or predicted and no claim about CNS exposure is made or implied; the barrier excludes essentially all large-molecule neurotherapeutics [@pardridge2005], and nothing here addresses that. ADMET-AI was used for ADMET description and refused to report outside its applicability domain; RDKit was used for chemical structure validation. No free energy is emitted by the neural predictor anywhere in this work: the Boltz-2 affinity head is fitted to pooled Ki, Kd, IC50 and EC50 labels and is never rendered as a thermodynamic quantity. The one free-energy-like quantity in the study is PRODIGY's contact-based dG, an empirical non-neural estimate that enters only as the PRODIGY discrimination ratio reported above.
