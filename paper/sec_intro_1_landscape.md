## Introduction

### The cholinergic account of cognitive decline, and its measured ceiling

The modern pharmacology of age-related cognitive decline begins with one hypothesis and one anatomical observation. Bartus and colleagues assembled the evidence that cholinergic function declines in the aged and demented central nervous system and that antimuscarinic blockade reproduces memory deficits in young subjects, while conceding in the same review that clinical attempts to exploit the mechanism had not succeeded [@bartus1982]. Whitehouse and colleagues supplied the substrate that year, reporting selective degeneration of greater than 75% of the cholinergic neurons of the nucleus basalis of Meynert [@whitehouse1982]. Four decades later the hypothesis is defended in essentially its original form, on the grounds that cholinesterase inhibitors remain among the few therapies proven clinically useful in Alzheimer's dementia [@hampel2018] — an argument from the absence of alternatives rather than from effect size.

The effect size is known and small. In a Cochrane review of 30 studies and 8257 participants, donepezil at 10 mg/day over 24 to 26 weeks separates from placebo by −2.67 points on the ADAS-Cog (95% CI −3.31 to −2.02; 5 studies, 1130 participants) and by +1.05 points on the MMSE (95% CI 0.73 to 1.37; 7 studies, 1757 participants), with no detectable benefit on neuropsychiatric symptoms or quality of life [@birks2018]. In 565 patients followed for up to three years it gave a mean advantage of 0.8 MMSE points over the first two years and no difference in entry to institutional care (42% versus 44% at three years, p=0.4) [@courtney2004]. Symptomatic cholinergic therapy is real, marginal, and has not improved in twenty years.

That ceiling drove the field elsewhere. Human genetics moved the centre of gravity to innate immunity: the largest Alzheimer's genome-wide association study to date, with 111,326 clinically diagnosed or proxy cases and 677,663 controls, reported 75 risk loci with pathway enrichment implicating amyloid and tau processing and highlighting microglia [@bellenguez2022], and innate-immune biology is now treated as a central axis of the disease [@heneka2025]. Synaptic and glutamatergic targets have been pursued in parallel, where receptor subtype rather than glutamatergic tone governs the direction of hippocampal plasticity — blockade of GluN2B-containing receptors abolishes long-term depression without affecting long-term potentiation, and preferential inhibition of GluN2A-containing receptors does the reverse [@liu2004] — and where GluN2 identity sets most of the pharmacological character of the assembled receptor [@hansen2018].

### The target registry, and what human genetics actually supports for each class

This study worked from a fixed registry of sixteen human proteins, each with a nominated structural domain and each graded for the strength of human genetic support for a role in cognition. Counting those grades over the sixteen entries, one is strong, two moderate, four weak, two contradictory and seven none. The registry is not a list of validated targets but of targets a designer might plausibly reach for.

**Cholinergic (ACHE, CHRNA7, CHRM1).** Acetylcholinesterase is among the best-characterised enzymes in structural biology: the catalytic triad sits at the bottom of a deep narrow gorge lined by fourteen aromatic residues [@sussman1991], and the gorge mouth carries a peripheral anionic site along which donepezil lies without contacting the triad [@kryger1999]. The source species of the structural template is not interchangeable — donepezil binds human AChE differently from the *Torpedo* enzyme [@cheung2012] — and peripheral-site-dependent acceleration of amyloid-beta fibril assembly, the property that motivates targeting the gorge mouth rather than the triad [@inestrosa1996], is a mechanism whose clinical relevance has not been established. ACHE is graded none. CHRNA7 encodes a homopentameric, exceptionally calcium-permeable receptor that desensitises faster than any other Cys-loop channel and was resolved structurally only in 2021 [@noviello2021]; the amyloid-beta interaction motivating most α7-directed design was reported at picomolar affinity [@wang2000], and whether that interaction reflects specific binding remains contested, with both agonist-like and antagonist-like effects reported for the same complex [@khan2010], while encenicline, the α7 agonist with the largest reported cognition trial cited here, reached an effect of Cohen's d = 0.257 (p=0.034) on its primary endpoint in 319 patients and lost significance at the higher dose [@keefe2015]. Graded weak. CHRM1 has the strongest efficacy signal of the three and the worst therapeutic index — xanomeline improved ADAS-Cog and CIBIC+ in 343 patients while causing 52% of high-dose patients to discontinue, with syncope in 12.6% [@bodick1997] — and selective allosteric chemistry has not made it tolerable [@nguyen2024]. Graded none.

**Neurotrophin receptors (NTRK1, NTRK2).** The mechanistic case for TrkB is strong in circuit physiology: knock-in mice mutated at the phospholipase C-gamma docking site of TrkB have impaired hippocampal long-term potentiation, whereas Shc-site mutants do not [@minichiello2002]. The structural target is well defined: the membrane-proximal Ig-like C2 domain (d5 in Trk nomenclature, Ig2 in UniProt's) is necessary and sufficient for neurotrophin binding, resolved crystallographically for NGF–TrkA-d5 [@wiesmann1999] and for neurotrophin-4/5–TrkB-d5 [@banfield2001]. The clinical record is not: recombinant BDNF failed its phase III endpoints in 1,135 patients with ALS [@the1999], and intracerebral AAV2-NGF in 49 participants produced no difference from sham on ADAS-cog at 24 months (P = .17) [@rafii2018]. Candidate-gene work on *NTRK2* in 216 cases and 244 controls was negative [@zeng2013], and the variant usually offered as support is on the ligand: across 82 studies of *BDNF* Val66Met, only 40 (48.8%) reported any association with a neurocognitive domain [@toh2018]. NTRK2 is graded weak, NTRK1 none.

**Microglial and innate immune (TREM2, TLR4).** TREM2 is the one entry graded strong, and it earns the grade twice: recessive loss of function causes Nasu-Hakola disease with early-onset dementia [@paloneva2002], and p.Arg47His confers an odds ratio of 2.92 for Alzheimer's disease (95% CI 2.09–4.09, P = 3.42 × 10⁻¹⁰) [@jonsson2013], replicated independently [@guerreiro2013] and joined by p.Arg62His (OR 1.67) in 85,133 subjects [@sims2017]. What the genetics does not supply is a pocket. The risk variants sit on a solvent-exposed surface of a single Ig-V domain [@kober2016], and the field disagrees about which face a therapeutic should engage — anionic lipids [@wang2015], apolipoprotein E [@atagi2015], or a distinct hydrophobic surface largely insensitive to the risk variants [@kober2020]. Causality does not guarantee benefit: the TREM2 agonist antibody AL002 confirmed cerebrospinal-fluid target engagement in 381 randomised participants with early Alzheimer's disease and missed its primary endpoint at every dose [@mummery2026]. TLR4 enters because amyloid-beta signals through a TLR4–TLR6 heterodimer whose assembly is regulated by signals from the scavenger receptor CD36 [@stewart2010]. Its recognition module is a hydrophobic MD-2 pocket shaped for acyl chains [@park2009]; eritoran, which occupies that pocket, failed a 1,961-patient phase 3 trial (28-day mortality 28.1% versus 26.9%, P = 0.59) [@opal2013], and the one selective small-molecule inhibitor binds the intracellular TIR domain instead [@matsunaga2011]. TLR4 is graded none.

**Glutamatergic (GRIN2A, GRIN2B, SLC1A2).** The GluN2 amino-terminal domain exerts genuine allosteric control, its conformational equilibrium setting channel open probability — approximately 0.5 for GluN2A-containing receptors against approximately 0.1 for GluN2B [@gielen2009] — but it is not one pocket: the phenylethanolamine site lies at the GluN1–GluN2B interface rather than within GluN2B [@karakas2011], and the GluN1–GluN2A equivalent has a distinct architecture hosting the high-affinity zinc site [@romerohernandez2016]. Human genetics points the wrong way for this domain: missense variants in the amino-terminal and ligand-binding domains produce predominantly loss of function [@strehlow2019], while *GRIN2B* de novo variants mix gain and loss [@platzer2017]. The clinical ceiling remains memantine, a non-selective open-channel blocker: 3.11 points on the Severe Impairment Battery (95% CI 2.42 to 3.92) at six months in moderate-to-severe disease, and probably no benefit in mild disease [@mcshane2019]. SLC1A2 encodes the astrocytic transporter responsible for up to approximately 95% of brain glutamate clearance [@pregnolato2019], is genetically necessary in mouse [@tanaka1997], and has one large negative trial of an expression-raising drug against it [@cudkowicz2014]. GRIN2A and GRIN2B are graded moderate, SLC1A2 weak.

**The remainder (GSK3B, KEAP1/NFE2L2, FZD8, NOS3, PTAFR).** These six were nominated on pathway logic rather than on human cognitive genetics, and the grading says so: GSK3B at the ATP-binding pocket of its kinase domain (none); KEAP1 and NFE2L2 as a pair — the Kelch β-propeller and the Neh2 ETGE/DLG degron that binds it — the only entries graded contradictory, meaning the human genetic evidence points in both directions; FZD8 at the Wnt/palmitoleate-binding cysteine-rich domain (none); NOS3 at the oxygenase domain (weak); PTAFR at the seven-transmembrane orthosteric pocket (none). For seven of its sixteen targets this registry offers no human genetic validation at all, and for two more the evidence points in both directions.

### Why peptides, and why the brain is the worst place to use them

The structural argument for peptides at protein–protein interfaces is straightforward: much of cellular regulation is mediated by short linear motifs in disordered regions, so the natural ligand for many interface pockets is itself a peptide [@van2014]. The modality is established, with over 60 peptide drugs approved in major markets and more than 150 in active clinical development [@lau2018].

The properties that make a peptide a good interface ligand — length, polarity, backbone hydrogen-bond donors, flexibility — are the properties that block brain entry and invite proteolysis. The blood–brain barrier is estimated to exclude essentially 100% of large-molecule neurotherapeutics and more than 98% of small-molecule drugs, with passive diffusion falling off sharply with molecular weight and hydrogen-bond donor count [@pardridge2005]. Unmodified peptides are cleared in minutes: somatostatin's few-minute plasma half-life rises to about 1.5 hours in octreotide only through truncation plus D-amino acid substitution [@werle2006]. Every mitigation trades against binding: PEGylation of gp41-derived fusion inhibitors bought up to 3.4-fold longer degradation half-life while every conjugate lost inhibitory activity [@danial2012]; excised α-helical motifs unfold outside their parent context and must have their helical shape chemically restored [@lee2014], yet stapling the canonical BimBH3 helix reduced rather than improved affinity and did not confer cell permeability [@okamoto2013]. Cell-penetrating and blood–brain barrier shuttle peptides are weaker than assumed: the energy-independent translocation once claimed for TAT and R9 was largely a fixation artefact [@richard2003], and among the few shuttles carrying capillary-depletion-corrected in vivo uptake data [@zhou2021], Angiopep-2 gives an 86-fold increase in brain influx coefficient [@thomas2009] that converted to an intracranial objective response rate of 15% by investigator assessment and 8% by independent review, missing the trial's preset rule [@kumthekar2020].

The closest precedent is a chimeric peptide of exactly the kind screened here. Nerinetide fuses the C-terminal PDZ-binding motif of NR2B to the TAT cell-penetrating peptide, dissociates NMDA receptors from PSD-95, and reduces infarct volume in rats [@aarts2002]. It failed phase III twice: 61.4% versus 59.2% reaching modified Rankin 0–2 in 1105 patients (adjusted risk ratio 1.04, 95% CI 0.96–1.14, p = 0.35) [@hill2020], and 45% versus 46% in a further 850 patients (odds ratio 0.97, 95% CI 0.72–1.30, p = 0.82) [@hill2025].

### What design success rates look like

Contemporary computational design reports its numbers, and they set the reference frame. The current stack pairs structure-conditioned backbone generation [@watson2023] with a sequence designer that recovers 52.4% of native sequence on native backbones against 32.9% for Rosetta [@dauparas2022]; the authors of the de novo binder pipeline state plainly that the overall design success rate is low, and that in-silico refolding of designs with AlphaFold2 or RoseTTAFold to check that the sequence adopts its designed structure and binds as designed raises success roughly ten-fold [@bennett2023]. For macrocyclic peptides, twenty or fewer designs per target yielded binders against all four targets attempted, with three crystal structures within 1.5 Å Cα RMSD of the design models [@rettie2025] and affinities of 1–10 µM for MCL1 and MDM2, 6 nM for the best anti-GABARAP macrocycle and below 10 nM for RbtA [@rettie2024]. Two of those four targets, in that macrocycle-design campaign, yielded only micromolar binders.

The peptides examined here did far less work than that. No generative model designed them, no sequence was optimised against an objective and no structure-based design software was used: the thirteen candidates screened are chimeric peptides, manually concatenated from published motifs, motif-like segments and one de novo helix, joined by GGGGS linkers — an element that adds conformational freedom rather than defined geometry, whose origin in the single-chain Fv already cost roughly six-fold in affinity relative to the parent Fab [@huston1988] and whose length selects oligomeric state and geometry rather than fixing the orientation of the fused motif [@holliger1993; @chen2013]. The provenance of the borrowed fragments is weak: of sixteen motif entries in the design record only seven carry a UniProt accession, and 31 of 35 candidates carry at least one of 22 fragments the record itself calls unattributed. No blood–brain-barrier permeability was measured or predicted for any candidate. Sequence-based classifiers for that property exist: one of them, B3Pred, reports 85.08% accuracy and AUROC 0.93 in cross-validation on its own curated training set [@kumar2021], and existing predictors of this class are reported as liable to fail in generalisation [@charoenkwan2022]. No benchmark identified while preparing this study covers chains of this length and composition.

The question asked here is therefore upstream of efficacy, selectivity and delivery: before any of those can be discussed, a designed peptide must be shown to engage its intended domain at all, and for reasons attributable to the arrangement of its residues rather than to their composition.
