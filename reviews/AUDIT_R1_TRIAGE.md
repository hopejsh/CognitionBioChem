## 1. VERDICT ON THE PROJECT

The science is real and the presentation layer is not yet safe to be public. I traced roughly 90 quantitative README claims to artefacts; the overwhelming majority reproduce to the digit, several were recomputed independently from raw rows rather than read from stored summaries (Spearman ρ = 0.8471 / p = 3.47e-05 in Slate #7, pooled across-seed SD 2.6615 in Slate #2, all 8 pre-registration SHA-256 hashes), and the project's negative results — including the ones that refute its own author's hypotheses — hold under scrutiny. The chirality sign fix, the grid clash search, Fisher/Wilson/Holm, the bootstrap pairing, and the mature-offset derivation were all verified against independent implementations and are correct. Against that: the repository went public with **no LICENSE at all** while redistributing 229 Apache-2.0 files copyrighted by Google LLC, committed ChEMBL CC BY-SA activity values, and 11,673 aa of UniProt sequence, under a README line reading "Private research. © 2026 Seung H. Jung" — a legal posture the author has no standing to assert over other people's work and which grants no rights to anyone over his own. Two headline public claims are false as stated: the "45 fabricated residues" figure is produced by a cross-attribution bug in `validate.py` (≥17 of the 45 are residues the project's own audit calls correct, or artefacts of the alias "M1" matching "TM1"), and the applicability-domain section asserts a 1000 Da rule was "deleted" when it is live at `admet.py:57` and fires as the first refusal reason. The site's own most-prominent element still tells every visitor "No structure prediction has been run in this repository" — and `verify_frontend.py:116` actively asserts that false string, so the suite guards the untruth. Net: the methodology is better than the repository's presentation of it, and right now a knowledgeable reader who checks two numbers will find one wrong. Fix the legal exposure and the four false public claims before this is cited; everything else is disclosure, relabelling, and about 200 lines of code.

Every short hash quoted in the README is real. Every study result's `prespec_hash` resolves to a registered plan.
- **The Holm implementation is correct in all eight copies.** `running = max(running, (K - rank) * p_sorted)` with 0-indexed rank is exactly the right multiplier; monotonicity is enforced; capped at 1.0. Verified by independent recomputation on two studies. There is no off-by-one.
- **Fisher exact matches scipy on 400 random 2×2 tables** (worst absolute difference 4.44e-16), including the extremes. **Wilson matches the closed form** to six decimals at k=0, k=n and interior points, and returns (0,1) rather than dividing by zero at n=0.
